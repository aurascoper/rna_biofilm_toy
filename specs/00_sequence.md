Two decisions were hiding in "3-D CPM" and they are separate. Dimension: the replicator-and-parasite results the toy paraphrases (Boerlijst and Hogeweg's hypercycles, Szabo and colleagues 2002 on limited dispersal selecting for efficiency and fidelity, Takeuchi and Hogeweg after them) are 2-D cellular automata, and spatial structure protects cooperators because a parasite can only spread along a front; in 3-D a site has 26 neighbours, fronts are thicker, and the protection weakens. Whether the 2-D result survives in 3-D is a real question and the right second experiment, but it is an experiment on a 2-D result that does not exist yet. Formalism: a cellular Potts model models things with extent (volume constraint, adhesion, a Hamiltonian, Metropolis acceptance); a ribozyme has none of that, so for molecules in a matrix the CA is correct and a CPM is ceremony. The CPM earns its place when the entity has a shape, which means a protocell, and then it is written from the Hamiltonian in about 300 lines in its own repository, not forked from Biofilms, whose code is welded to its evidence discipline, checkpoint schema and claims tests. Electrostatics is added only when a genotype variable pays a cost or gains a yield from it; a potential nothing reads is the same inert passenger as a nutrient field nothing consumed. Rendering is solved: any field is one more array in a .vti and the existing viewer reads it. The molecular lane (03) is independent of all of this.

Two triage pages sit beside the lanes: specs/06 maps the chapters of classical electrodynamics to what, if anything, reads them at each lane's pitch and timescale, and specs/07 does the same for evolutionary game theory, decision theory and quorum sensing. So the order is: fixed 2-D toy to one result with a prediction first; 3-D CA to test whether it survives; CPM only when a protocell appears; electrostatics when something reads it; rendering through the existing pipeline.

Three further records, so they survive the conversation that produced them.

First, pitch and energy are coupled, and this is a gate on any lane that carries a dose field. Once the pitch and the attenuation or range coefficient are both declared, the gradient across the domain is determined, not chosen, and a prediction about depth is vacuous if the field is flat across the lattice. The worked case is the shipped Biofilms field I(r) = I0 exp(-kappa r / N) with kappa = 2 and N = 40, which is mu = 0.05 per site and an axis-to-wall ratio of e, 2.72, over R = 20 sites. Ask what photon energy that is and the pitch falls out. Using mass attenuation coefficients for water recalled from NIST XCOM (0.0863 cm2/g at 662 keV, 0.0632 at 1.25 MeV, 0.137 at 208 keV; all to be verified against XCOM before the note below is written), the implied pitch is about 5.8 mm per site for a 662 keV photon (a 23 cm domain) and about 7.9 mm per site for 1.25 MeV (32 cm). Run the other way, at 1 um per site, the axis-to-wall ratio for MeV gamma is 1.0001: no gradient. Only photons near 1 keV give a 2.7 ratio across 40 um, and those are stopped in microns and are not what a reactor emits. So the shipped gradient is quantitatively consistent with reactor-relevant gamma at pool scale, tens of centimetres, and is not realizable at biofilm scale at any plausible energy. This does not contradict the earlier null result, which was about the magnitude of beta_ion; it is a separate statement. It also says where structure at biofilm-to-millimetre scale comes from: beta emitters, whose endpoint ranges are millimetres in water, not gamma; a beta emitter with a few-hundred-keV endpoint and 100 to 200 keV gamma lines gives a gradient through its betas and none through its gammas at that scale.
