Emitter class, declared on line one: short-range particles from a radioactive mineral surface, that is alpha, beta and fission fragments from the decay and fission inventory, not bulk gamma. The dose field is therefore a near-surface boundary layer set by distance from the surface and falling off over tens of micrometres, and D(z) is a surface-source range falloff, not exp(-mu z). Status: spec only, no code. Gate: starts only after the 2-D toy has one measured result with a prediction committed first (PREDICTION.md) and after Biofilms PRs #72 and #73 and the manuscript revision. Its own repository when it starts.

Declared lattice: N = 40 sites per axis, pitch 1 um per site, so a 40 um domain (0.004 cm). Water density 1.000 g/cm3 (NIST XCOM/ESTAR material 276). Seconds per step: UNDECLARED until the rate constants below force a choice; nothing may default it to a number. Gradient threshold, declared here rather than buried in prose: the domain must span at least 0.1 of the emitter's range or 1/e length for a depth gradient to count as resolvable. This is a judgement call and is written down so it can be argued with.

Beta energies, for the record, from LNHB tables opened 2026-10-06: the 496.8(8) keV endpoint belongs to a branch at 79.45(29) percent whose per-branch average is 147.75(27) keV; the 2278.7(16) keV endpoint belongs to a branch at 99.983(7) percent with average 926.7(8) keV. The decay-averaged means are the quantities a feasibility row should carry; the LNHB tables print per-branch averages only, so they are UNDECLARED, and the rows above bracket a mean-energy-class electron instead. A beta spectrum has no single range: the endpoint electron's CSDA range is six to eight times the mean-energy electron's, and for the 497 keV endpoint spectrum that puts the mean-energy class at 0.14 to 0.19 of the domain, above the declared 0.1 threshold, while the endpoint is at 0.023. So that spectrum straddles the gate and the energy column is what makes the row honest; the 2279 keV spectrum is flat at both energies. Read at 1 um per site: alpha spans 0.92 of its range across the domain, a real gradient at a real pitch and the physically realizable Oklo case; the 497 keV endpoint electron spans 0.02 and the 1.25 MeV photon 0.00025, both flat, so a gradient prediction at this pitch with a gamma coefficient would be unfalsifiable. To put a beta emitter's endpoint range across the domain the pitch would be about 45 um per site (497 keV) or about 275 um per site (2279 keV); for the 662 keV photon about 2.9 mm per site, which is a pool, not a biofilm.

Model, stated in one paragraph. A 40-cubed lattice at the declared pitch with a mineral surface at z = 0 and the dose field D(z) given by the chosen emitter's range falloff (alpha: the ASTAR CSDA profile; to be tabulated from the stopping power, not fitted). Replicators occupy sites and carry two heritable traits, a shielding value s in [0, 1] and a replication fidelity f in [0, 1]. Monomers are a continuous field with conservative diffusion (the toy's scheme, extended to 26 or 6 neighbours as declared) and a source term proportional to local dose through a G-value. Replication consumes a declared number of monomers and copies the parent with error rate 1 - f. Strand cleavage removes a replicator at a rate proportional to local dose times (1 - s). Shielding and fidelity each cost something declared, or the page says why they are free and what that implies. Single observable: the depth z* at which net per-replicator fitness (births minus deaths per replicator per step, averaged over a declared window) crosses zero, as a function of surface dose rate D0.

Prediction, written before any run, with the reason it could fail. There is an interior optimum depth, not a monotone preference for the surface or for depth. Mechanism: monomer supply is proportional to dose but replication is gated by a threshold and the monomer field is smoothed by diffusion, so the benefit of dose saturates and spreads; cleavage is linear in dose and local, so the cost does not saturate. Near the surface cost exceeds benefit, far from it supply is too low to reach threshold, and in between there is a band where net fitness is positive. The band should move deeper as D0 rises. It fails for the right reason if, with the forms above and the traced constants, net fitness is monotone in depth, which would mean the saturation argument is wrong at these rates or the diffusion length is larger than the range falloff length. Arms, same seeds: full; cleavage off (shadow), in which the prediction is that fitness is monotone increasing toward the surface; and supply off, in which the lattice should go extinct at a rate set by cleavage alone. The feasibility gate above runs before the prediction is registered, and a flat field voids the registration.
