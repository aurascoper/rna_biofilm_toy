Molecular lane: solved electrostatics for albumin and warfarin. Status: spec only, no code. Gate: after Ellen's copy and PRs #72 and #73. Independent of the lattice lanes; a separate project at angstrom scale, where a binding pocket really does feel unscreened partial charges and where there is no ecosystem.

What it replaces. Coulomb_Warfarin.R (github.com/aurascoper/Coulomb_Warfarin.R) is a visualization with electrostatics in its name and comments rather than in its computation: calculate_coulomb_force is defined and never called; generate_electron_cloud sums isotropic Gaussians on a 50-cubed grid around eight hand-placed points under a comment that says "using Maxwell's Equations", with the four equations sitting above it as comments nothing evaluates; the docking is runif offsets of 15 to 18 angstrom added to coordinates; the ligand is eight points rather than warfarin's 23 heavy atoms; and the electron-cloud branch calls scatter3D and ramp.col from plot3D, which the script never loads, so that checkbox errors. A fine sketch of an interactive 3-D molecular view, not a field solve.

The honest version. Run PDB2PQR on 1AO6 (human serum albumin) to assign charges and protonation states at a declared pH and force field; run APBS to solve the Poisson-Boltzmann equation for the electrostatic potential of albumin in electrolyte at a declared ionic strength and temperature; look at the Sudlow site I pocket in the field it actually has. APBS writes an OpenDX grid, which converts to a .vti in a few lines, and the existing viewer reads .vti. Warfarin is then a ligand with real partial charges placed by a docking tool, not by runif; the warfarin-bound albumin structure in the PDB (to be identified and cited during execution, not from memory) is the reference for the pocket. A weekend, reusing the pipeline, delivering the thing the old repository's name promised.

Declared on the page before any run: PDB entry and chain, pH, force field, ionic strength, temperature, grid spacing and extent, solute and solvent dielectric, each with its source. Anything not located is UNDECLARED.

What it borrows from the two repositories that were offered as references: nothing. Wannier_Coulomb (danis-b) evaluates bare Coulomb matrix elements U, V and J by Monte Carlo over Wannier functions that wannier90 produced from a DFT calculation of a periodic crystal; its output is an on-site repulsion in electron-volts for a Hubbard model, two regimes below any lattice here, and it needs a DFT and a Wannierisation that do not exist in this project. The one conceivable bridge, a Hubbard parameter for eumelanin, would be a separate multi-month solid-state program feeding a tight-binding model, not a CPM term. molecule (colombolab) is a deep-learning classifier for allosteric versus orthosteric kinase ligands built on Amber MD descriptors and RDKit fingerprints; it has no electrostatics, no field, and inputs that are not available here. Both say the same useful thing: a Coulomb integral is something one computes rather than names.
