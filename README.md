This directory is a standalone sandbox for spatial protection under an assumed shielding rule in a 2-D lattice toy, transcribed from an LLM-written guide and repaired after audit. It contains no RNA, polymerization or chemistry and validates no historical origin scenario. CSV provenance is qualified in data/PROVENANCE.md and the dated artifact audit. It shares nothing with ~/Developer/Biofilms. Pure numpy; the tests need pytest (python3.13 on this machine has it).

The paste's engine had four defects. First, damage was applied to the deep-copied snapshot organism and the live grid was never decremented, so lysis was unreachable and the CSV's monotone population curve is that bug, not an ecosystem. Second, the replication weight used dx times the flux difference for a field that varies only in y, which rewards the (+1,+1) and (-1,-1) diagonals and not the gradient. Third, injection and the death return were clamped at 100 with no record of what the clamp discarded, so the guide's mass-invariance formula could not be audited; from step 4 on, roughly 1850 of every 2025 injected monomers vanish into the clamp. Fourth, dx was not unwrapped at the x seam, so a seam-crossing slot had dx of 49, the exponent hit the clamp at 20, and the move got weight e^20; harmless in the CSV run only because the colony never reaches the seam in 100 steps.

engine_original.py keeps all four on purpose. Its engine functions are verbatim, with the restored line breaks listed in TRANSCRIPTION.md, and only the runner and CSV writer were added (in run.py). The earlier reviewer's populations of 298 and 293 came from a copy that unwrapped the seam displacement; this file does not, and its own numbers are recorded below.

engine.py changes exactly these things. Damage, lysis and the parent copy act on the live organism; the snapshot is read only for neighbour occupancy and purine, so newborns neither shield nor take damage in the step they are born, and an organism that lysed earlier in a step still shields later-processed neighbours. A cell vacated by lysis is still occupied in the snapshot, so it is not available to a birth until the next step; that is the original's semantics, kept. The lattice is a cylinder: x wraps, y does not, five neighbours on rows 0 and 49 and eight elsewhere. This removes the seam cliff that a linear field has on a torus (row 49 to row 0 would drop by 2.45, making the top row a near-deterministic teleporter), rather than papering over it; the guide's sentence that toroidal boundaries eliminate edge artifacts was false for its own field. The alignment is a direction cosine: the unit displacement dotted with the field gradient, with dx unwrapped on the periodic axis. For this field a +y step gets radiotropism times 0.05, a diagonal gets that over root two, and an x step gets exactly zero. The other defensible form, the plain flux difference, would give a diagonal the full row gain; the choice was made by the owner and means "how well the move points", not "how much flux it gains". np.gradient uses one-sided differences at x = 0 and 49 and does not know x is periodic, which is harmless only because this field's x derivative is exactly zero. The diffusion assert is now D <= 1 with its real name, the non-negativity bound (outflux is at most D times the cell's content for this divide-by-degree scheme), instead of a CFL bound borrowed from a different scheme. Operator correction, 2026-10-07: the engine transfers only downhill, using the giver’s degree. Removing the downhill guard defines a different, unguarded reference, with four matrix updates per ordered link and symmetric coefficient D(1/n_i + 1/n_j). Its explicit-Euler stability bound is about 0.676798 at D=1 on the 5 by 4 diagnostic cylinder. That is neither a linearisation nor a stability result for the engine’s guarded update. Column sums govern total mass conservation; row sums govern preservation of a uniform field. Symmetry, both sum checks and agreement with the unguarded routine are separate requirements. Reject asymmetry before `Symmetric(M)`, which otherwise uses one triangle to analyze a different matrix. The local ignored Julia practice file retains its exercise stubs; it is not a completed implementation.

The mass ledger has five terms, each a measured delta, so the identity holds by construction and not by luck: injected_gross (always 2025), inject_clip (what the vent clamp discarded), diffusion_clip (the signed sum of pre minus post around np.clip), birth_sink (15 per birth) and death_return (what was actually added after the death clamp, so the death clamp is folded into this term rather than being a sixth). Every step, M(t) - M(t-1) equals injected_gross - inject_clip - diffusion_clip - birth_sink + death_return, asserted to 1e-8 (the measured float residual is about 4e-12 at M near 14000). diffusion_clip is provably zero for this scheme: a cell below 100 can receive at most 0.24 times its headroom and a cell at 100 receives nothing, and outflux never exceeds 0.15 of the content; it is kept as a column anyway so that the identity does not depend on the geometry. births and deaths are logged as integers so birth_sink equals 15 times births and the death clamp shows as death_return below 5 times deaths. Three spatial observables follow, computed in the same loop that counts the population: mean_occupied_degree (occupied Moore neighbours per organism on the cylinder), clustering_index, and mean_y (mean row of the occupied cells). The index is the mean occupied degree divided by what uniform random placement of the same number of organisms would give, which is the mean available degree over the occupied sites (5 on the two edge rows, 8 inside) times (N minus 1)/2499; it is 1.0 for a random scatter and six to eight for a solid blob at these populations, and nan below two organisms. One caution that decides how the index may be used: at fixed shape it falls like 2499/N (an interior strip of height h has mean occupied degree 8 minus 6/h while the random expectation grows with N; measured 12.6, 7.3, 4.6, 2.4 for h = 3, 6, 10, 20), and radiation lowers N, so comparing arms at step 100 alone would manufacture the predicted sign. Arms are compared on the index-against-population curve at shared populations, and the raw mean occupied degree is in the file so anyone can re-derive. The fixed CSV therefore has 15 columns, and its first row is step 0, the seeded configuration before any step, with the ledger columns at zero.

To run:

    python -I audit_telemetry.py data/simulation_telemetry.csv
    python audit_artifacts.py --width 50 --height 50 data/simulation_telemetry.csv --json
    python run.py --engine original --seed 1 --steps 100 --purpose verification --out out/NEW_original_s1.csv
    python run.py --engine fixed --seed 1 --steps 100 --purpose verification --field-scale 1 --decay-scale 1 --out out/NEW_fixed_s1.csv
    python3.13 -m pytest tests -q

The fixed engine takes two knobs. --field-scale multiplies the radiation field before its gradient is taken, so it scales the damage and the tropism birth bias together: at scale 4 the +y to -y weight ratio at radiotropism 0.5 is 1.221, a 22 percent per-birth edge rather than the 5 percent at scale 1, while top-row damage is 0.49 per step. --decay-scale multiplies the decay constant only. The five arms in PREDICTION.md are the field ladder 0, 0.25, 1, 4 at decay 1, plus the attribution arm field 1 / decay 0, in which the gradient is present and damage is off, so its mean_y shift is the birth-bias contribution alone and is subtracted from the full arm's. At field 0 every slot weight is exactly 1 and deaths are exactly 0 for every seed, which is why population and deaths are not observables against that arm. Measured at seed 1: only 50 of 178 births have more than one empty slot, and at field 4 the per-choice probability shift is 2 to 5 percent, so about one choice per run is altered by the field; the seed-1 runs at decay 0 for field 0, 1 and 4 are identical cell for cell. The tropism bias is therefore a near-zero effect at 100 steps and mean_y moves through deaths; PREDICTION.md records this before any stage run.

Every numeric band in tests/test_engine.py was measured with the committed code on 2026-10-06 and written in as the measured value, not chosen: the random-scatter index over 200 replicates of 300 organisms has mean 0.99615, sd 0.07178, range 0.8252 to 1.1697, so the test band is mean plus or minus four sd; the null-arm mean_y over seeds 1 to 10 after 30 steps has mean 24.985, sd 0.112; the edge-row ratio 1.03896 is the same for a 50-column and a 20-column block because the expectation uses each occupied site's own available degree.

The audit of the recovered CSV (provenance in data/PROVENANCE.md) gives births 26, 10 and 3 for steps 1 to 3, inferred from mass and matching the population differences exactly; the identity breaks at step 4 with 1227 monomers clipped; the population is monotone non-decreasing over all 100 steps; and the mean purine ratio ranges from 1.49983 to 1.50035 across 177 births, which at a mutation rate of 0.02 is about 3.5 mutation events of plus or minus 0.05. That flat line alone cannot establish absence of selection; the low mutation supply limits measurable genotype change.

Why the genotype columns cannot be observables, stated as a derivation rather than an observation: the survival time of an organism that never replicates is exp(k u S)/(flux times decay) with k = 0.25, own purine u and neighbours' purine sum S, so d(ln t)/du = k S exactly, independent of flux. With eight neighbours at purine 1.5 that is 3.0 per unit purine, and one +0.05 mutation buys 16.2 percent more survival time (0, 3.8, 7.8, 16.2 percent for 0, 2, 4, 8 neighbours). Selection is strong. The columns are frozen purely by mutation supply, three or four events per run at MUTATION_RATE 0.02, and the only lever that would move them is MUTATION_RATE, which is a separate decision with its own pre-registration. The zero at no neighbours says purine is worth nothing to an isolated organism, so purine and clustering are not separable traits.

The guide's claim that the mean purine ratio should approach 9.0 asymptotically is unsupported for two reasons, and the second is the stronger. After the health fix there is selection for purine through shielding and nothing costs it, so the mean can drift upward within the clamp; 9.0 is a bound, not an established attractor. And the mutation supply is 0.02 per birth at plus or minus 0.05, three or four events across a whole run, so no observable movement is possible at this horizon under any selection.
